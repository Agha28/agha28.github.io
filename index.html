About

I study at the Indian Institute of Science, where I'm part of Dr. Biswaranjan Behera's group at the Centre for High Energy Physics. My interests sit between neutrino physics, particle detectors and computational astroparticle physics.

Neutrinos interact so weakly with matter that most of them pass straight through the Earth. The same property lets them escape the Sun's core and the interior of a collapsing massive star, carrying information that light cannot. Detecting them takes very large detectors and careful calculation of what each one should see.

My work is on that calculation. Given a neutrino source, a detector material and a set of assumptions about neutrino physics, I compute how many events the detector would record, in which reaction channels and at what energies, and whether that is enough to tell one hypothesis from another. I'm aiming for a PhD in neutrino or high-energy physics.

Research

Centre for High Energy Physics, IISc · Supervisor: Dr. Biswaranjan Behera · 2025 to present

The group works with deuterated liquid scintillator detectors. Deuterium lets a detector respond to several kinds of neutrino reactions at once, which makes it useful for separating flavours. My research has three connected parts.

Building the event-rate model

The starting point was a full event-rate pipeline for supernova neutrinos in these detectors, covering neutrino flux, deuteron cross sections, detector light response and the charged-current, neutral-current and elastic-scattering channels. I built it from the published description of an earlier study (Chauhan, Dasgupta and Datar, 2021), and it agrees on neutral-current rates to within 1 to 3%. An initial 17 to 20% gap in the charged-current rates came from coarse sampling in the printed cross-section tables, and fell below 4% with the original tables. I then extended the model to recoil-neutron spectra and secondary scintillation.

Neutrino mass ordering across eight detectors

Using that model, I compared eight detector configurations, from water and heavy water to doped and loaded scintillators, on how well a future galactic supernova could reveal the ordering of the neutrino masses. Switching to time-dependent supernova fluxes with adaptive time-binning recovers sensitivity from the early accretion phase that whole-burst analyses wash out. The comparison also showed that the choice of supernova simulation changes the answer more than most detector choices do.

Solar neutrinos and a possible sterile neutrino

I compute the survival probability of boron-8 solar neutrinos under the standard three-flavour picture and under a model with an extra sterile neutrino, by evolving the quantum state through realistic solar density profiles. The calculation identifies the energy window where the two models differ most, and the exposure needed to distinguish them. It also covers the day-night asymmetry caused by neutrinos crossing the Earth, in a 1 kton detector. A manuscript is in preparation.

Open-source software

SNEWPY: diffuse supernova neutrino background module

SNEWS2 Collaboration · 2026 to present · pull request under review

SNEWPY is the Python package used by the supernova neutrino community to turn supernova simulations into predicted detector signals. I'm contributing a module for the diffuse supernova neutrino background, the faint, steady flux from all past supernovae in the universe. It computes the redshift-dependent flux from cosmological core-collapse rates and mass-weighted progenitor spectra, with flavour conversion for both mass orderings, and it reuses SNEWPY's existing flux, flavour-transformation and rate-calculation tools instead of duplicating them. It is backed by a 21-test suite and has been revised through the maintainers' code review.

Independent projects

I learn a method by implementing it end to end, checking it against published numbers, and writing down honestly where it does not match. Three of these projects are on GitLab.

Diffuse supernova neutrino background sensitivity

Based on Li, Vagins and Wurm (2022) · 2025 to 2026

A first-principles calculation of how long Super-Kamiokande with gadolinium and JUNO must run to detect the background. It matches the published detection table to rounding precision, and adds new sensitivity maps over the neutrino energy and the black-hole fraction. A remaining 15% difference in signal rate is documented, not corrected by hand.

Directional solar-neutrino detection in Borexino

Based on Basilico et al. (2023) · 2026

A simulated detector that uses the faint directional Cherenkov light in a scintillation signal to tell which events point back to the Sun. A Bayesian fit returns the signal count with credible intervals, and a calibration check covered the true value in 73% of 30 trials against a nominal 68%.

Finding GW150914 in public data

Python, PyCBC, GWOSC · 2026

A complete matched-filtering pipeline from raw data to detection. It recovers the first gravitational-wave event independently in both LIGO detectors, with signal-to-noise ratios of 17.55 and 13.10, within about 30 ms of the published merger time.
